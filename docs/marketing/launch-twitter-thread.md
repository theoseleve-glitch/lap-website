# Launch Twitter thread

An eight-tweet thread for the LAP public-beta launch on 2026-07-23. Mirrors the founder-direct posture of the T212 Reddit drafts and T214 demo script — no pitch openers, no fabricated user counts, no leaderboards-and-fireworks. Each tweet is drafted at ≤280 chars (Twitter's per-tweet cap); URLs auto-shorten to t.co (~23 chars) which keeps the count safely under regardless of original length. Tweets are numbered with `/N` suffixes so readers can verify the full thread is intact even when retweets clip context.

## Tweet 1 — Hook

One per-driver record. Across iRacing, ACC, AMS2, rFactor 2, AND real-track sessions from a phone GPS or RaceBox. Hand-curated coaching, no language-model narrative. Public beta opens 2026-07-23. /1

## Tweet 2 — The fragmentation problem

Most telemetry tools sit inside one ecosystem. iRacing has its layer, ACC has another, real track-day captures live somewhere else again. The driver record never crosses platforms — improvement gets re-discovered every time. /2

## Tweet 3 — The cross-platform claim

LAP captures iRacing, ACC, AMS2, and rFactor 2 sessions on one per-driver record. The same brake-zone shape detector runs at Suzuka hairpin whether the lap was virtual on Tuesday or real on Sunday. Cross-platform from day one. /3

## Tweet 4 — Real-track ingestion

Real-track captures come from a phone GPS clipped to the dash or a RaceBox unit. No professional telemetry rig needed. The per-driver record extends across sim and real-world racing — the shape transfers, even when the magnitudes don't. /4

## Tweet 5 — Rule library

The coaching is hand-curated by humans, not generated by a language model. Every diagnosis traces to a written rule the team maintains in public — predicate, trigger, prescription, success criterion, all four. /5

## Tweet 6 — Drills with measurable criteria

Each diagnosis comes with a named drill and a measurable success criterion. e.g. "Hold full brake pressure 0.3s longer through apex on 4 of 5 attempts." Re-evaluate next session. No leaderboards, no setup widgets, no gamification. /6
